# Separate Prose Revision From Everyday Plain Writing

Chose a dedicated `revise-for-naturalness` skill over putting the full workflow in
`write-plainly`. The owner requested a reusable skill after an extended
manuscript revision and asked whether parts should also enter `write-plainly`.

The useful process involved comparing revisions, learning from the author's
corrections, applying that calibration section by section, and making a
second pass for the same kinds of failure. Ordinary replies shouldn't load
that workflow or begin a calibration interview.

## What Carries Across Documents

Naturalness depends on the reader following a thought without repairing its
logic. Sentence length and repeated shapes can suggest places to inspect,
but don't establish that a passage is weak. The same revision can be smoother
and less faithful if it drops a causal step or narrows a concept.

The skill therefore keeps meaning, natural reading, and significance
connected. Its examples include useful long sentences and deliberate
parallelism. They teach decisions rather than a new preferred sentence shape.
An acceptable passage can stay unchanged.

The originating manuscript's terminology and story facts remain example
context. They aren't instructions for other authors. Punctuation preferences
and word-count targets weren't promoted into universal revision rules.

## Assessment of Write-Plainly

Keep its broad trigger and low-cost everyday use. Its existing requirements
to preserve evidence, exact terms, qualifications, and requirements remain
valuable. This change leaves its files untouched; the owner requested
consideration of changes there, not yet their implementation.

Recommended adjustments to consider:

- Qualify “one thought in each sentence.” Prefer a sentence that carries one
  coherent thought, including the cause, contrast, or qualification needed
  to understand it. Splitting can hide that connection.
- Qualify the ban on repetition. Remove empty repetition while retaining
  parallel definitions, stable terms, and useful teaching returns.
- Expand preservation beyond listed facts and requirements to include scope,
  causal relationships, and whose belief or feeling is being described.
- Extend its human-meaning check: state the consequence that makes a claim
  matter, using supplied evidence, without manufacturing emotion or certainty.

The first two proposals would change existing guidance, not just add missing
coverage. The plan-work-unit skill also carries a short “one thought per
sentence” fallback. An authorized update should assess that fallback rather
than silently leave two versions of the principle.

These proposals preserve the earlier decisions to offer general writing
guidance, keep substantial examples outside the entry point, and put faithful
meaning ahead of brevity. See the
[original skill decision](2026-08-28-0801-write-plainly-skill.md) and the
[evidence-preservation correction](2026-09-04-2340-writing-evidence.md).

Revisit the split if using both skills creates competing editorial behavior,
or if ordinary writing repeatedly needs the full calibration workflow.
